# The rise of Open-Source AI

There are more and more open-source AI models available.  DeepSeek just release version 4 of its model.  DeepSeek is open-source (well, its weights are freely available, not the training data that was used, nor the process of how it was trained!).  Still, DeepSeek is just an example, and one that competes with the latest versions of OpenAI, Google Gemini, and Anthropic.  DeepSeek is SotA (State of the Art).

Whether you trust DeepSeek or not is another issue.  One thing I can tell you though is that it rivals the best out there in evaluations.

## Collapse of AI financing?

Is this just a clever ploy of China to collapse the US AI economy?  The model is certainly cheaper to run that the equivalents.  OpenAI's true cost of ChatGPT 5.5 is estimated to be around $US180 per one million tokens.

A lot of the big AI companies are now changing from a flat fee to a per token fee.  They need to at least recuperate what they're spending.  Problem is, how do you compete with something that is "free" (or subsidized by China)?

Are we prepared to pay a 5 to 12 times price increase for using AI?  How will a pay for model work for good but expensive AI, when there are much cheaper comparable alternatives?  Is this how the AI bubble pops?

## Democratization of AI

I do believe that AI needs to be in the hands of the public, i.e. Open-Source AI.  That is not to say that there isn't any room for improvement.  The argument is the same as the old software argument.  Before "home" and "personal" computers software was out of the hands of ordinary people, inaccessible (there were technological challenges too).  
But I argue that what is happening now is similar.

You might argue the same happened with open-source software.  It made it possible for millions of us to read code, see how it's done, and build on the shoulders of giants.
